Account Owner vs Annotator: Which Role Fits You?

A direct comparison of Starkworth's two roles — Account Owner and Annotator — covering what each involves, who they suit, and how to decide.

Introduction

Starkworth's model has two distinct participants, and confusing the two is the most common source of misplaced expectations. An Account Owner is verified and signs a profit-sharing agreement, then has their account professionally managed. An Annotator registers, gets approved, and performs the actual annotation work for weekly pay. Neither role is "better" — they're structurally different arrangements suited to different people.

Who This Applies To

This guide is for anyone unsure which path to take. If you're weighing whether to apply as an Annotator or go through Account Owner verification, read this before starting either process — it will save you from starting the wrong one.

Why It Matters

Picking the right role up front matters because the two onboarding paths are different (identity verification and live screening for Account Owners; registration and review for Annotators), and switching later means starting over. Understanding the distinction also prevents a common misconception: that one role is simply a "better-paid" version of the other. They aren't comparable that way.

Step-by-Step Process

Both paths ultimately lead to the same place — a signed, individual agreement and weekly payouts — but the roads there differ. Account Owners move through explanation, verification, screening, then signing. Annotators move through registration, review, then approval. See the dedicated step-by-step guides for each: Become an Account Owner or Register as an Annotator.

What You Need to Get Started

  • To become an Account Owner: valid government ID and a laptop for live screening
  • To become an Annotator: your contact details, country of residence, and preferred payment method
  • For either path: time to actually read the agreement before signing anything

Typical Timeline

Account Owner onboarding runs through several sequential steps (explanation, verification, screening, signing) that typically take longer than Annotator onboarding, which is a single registration form followed by a 48-hour review window.

What Determines Your Terms

Starkworth doesn't use fixed public pricing tiers — every Account Owner and Annotator works under an individually signed agreement. The figures below explain what shapes those terms, not a price list.

Account Owner earnings are a percentage share defined in their individual agreement. Annotator earnings are based on task performance and input, also defined at registration. Neither is a fixed public rate — see Understanding Your Profit-Sharing Percentage for more detail.

Ongoing Support

Still torn between the two? Chat with Support or use the Contact form — describing your situation (time availability, comfort with verification, whether you want active task work or managed passive involvement) gets you a direct answer faster than guessing.

Example Scenario

Illustrative example — not a real customer case study

Someone with a full-time job who wants supplemental income without daily task commitments is more likely to fit the Account Owner path, since the account is professionally managed on their behalf. Someone with consistent free time who enjoys detail-oriented work and wants direct weekly pay for hours of effort is more likely to fit the Annotator path.
