Starkworth vs Freelancing Directly on an AI Platform

How a Starkworth-managed account compares to freelancing directly and independently on an AI data-annotation platform.

Introduction

Some AI annotation platforms let individuals sign up and work completely independently, with no manager or intermediary. Starkworth is a different model: it manages accounts under signed profit-sharing agreements, splitting the work of account management and task execution between an Account Owner and Annotators.

Neither model is objectively better — they suit different people. Understanding the difference helps you set the right expectations before you commit to either.

Key Points

  • Independent freelancing: one person owns, manages, and works the account directly, with 100% of earnings (minus platform fees) going to them.
  • Starkworth model: account management and task execution are split between an Account Owner and Annotators, each earning a defined share.
  • Starkworth adds a signed agreement, verification, and structured onboarding that pure freelancing typically doesn't require.
  • Starkworth is a service provider and manager — not the AI platform itself, and not affiliated with, sponsored by, or endorsed by it.

What You Give Up, What You Gain

Under Starkworth's model, an Account Owner gives up doing the day-to-day annotation work and a portion of earnings, in exchange for not having to manage tasks, quality assurance, or performance optimisation themselves. An Annotator gives up account ownership in exchange for task-based weekly pay without needing to run an account or sign a profit-sharing split.

Starkworth's operations are conducted on external, third-party freelance platforms it doesn't control or represent.
